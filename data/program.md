## Race Autopsy — 200 Breaststroke

The last 50 is the whole story.

**50 splits — as reported vs target (seconds), and stroke counts by 50:**
| Split | Your % of race | Target % of race |
|---|---|---|
| 1st 50 | 70% (28s region) | 68% |
| 2nd 50 | 76% (31s region) | 74% |
| 3rd 50 | 86% (34s region) | 80% |
| 4th 50 | 100% (37s region) | 87% |

- PB race stroke counts: 17 · 19 · 21 · 25
- Last meet stroke counts: 19 · 21 · 23 · 28
- +3 strokes on the final 50 = stroke length collapsing, not technique missing.
- Time to 15m: ~6m fresh off a dive vs ~13m surfacing on the last 50 (weak pullout).

**Do the math:** hold 37.5 on the last 50 → 2:21.5. Verdict: engine and pacing are real (31/36 shows up every race). Under lactate, force per stroke collapses — a strength-reserve and fuel problem, fixable on land.

### Diagnosis

| Weakness | Why | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Start: never ahead at the flags | Squat ~60kg working at ~63kg BW — block force is the limiter, not reaction. | Max-strength waves (Sep–Oct), then seated box jumps + trap-bar jumps = pure dead-start explosion. |
| Last-50 underwater dies (~6m) | Trunk stiffness and leg drive fade in lactate — you float up and lose the pullout. | "Lactic core" finishers: hollow holds + jumps immediately after main lifts — stiffness trained tired. |
| +3 strokes on the final 50 | Force reserve too small + under-fueled: each stroke sits too close to your max. | Muscle-gain window (Jul–Aug) + strength waves raise the ceiling — same speed at lower % effort. |

### Keep doing
- Balanced body — every limb 102–107% on InBody
- Front-half discipline: 31/36 shows up every race
- Solid turns when fresh
- 400 IM engine — aerobic base is not your problem

## Why It Looks Like a 200 BR Plan — One Engine, Five Events

| Trained quality | Where it pays off |
|---|---|
| Block & leg power | Biggest % gain in the 50 BR (the start is a third of that race) and the 100 BR — plus every start you own, both IMs included. |
| Pull / pullout strength | Every breaststroke wall: 4 pullouts in the 200 BR, the BR legs of both IMs — and the same lats power fly and freestyle pulling. |
| Core stiffness under fatigue | The last-50 fix in the 200 BR is the identical fix for the free leg of your 200 IM — holding body line when you're dying. |
| Turn & wall power | The 200 IM has 7 turns and 3 stroke transitions — hip-thrust and jump work pays off there more than in any other event. |
| Shoulder package | Keeps the fly and backstroke legs of both IMs fast and healthy through 9 swims a week. Insurance that doubles as speed. |

The 400 IM's engine is built in the pool — coaches own that. Land's job is force, walls and stiffness, and those feed all five events. The 200 BR anchors the design only because it's where the most time is losable.

## The Muscle Question — Yes, and the Window Is Now

- Fat mass (7.6 kg) is below the normal floor (7.9–15.8). Getting "skinnier" means eating into muscle — the thing that moves you.
- **Window closes Aug 30.** Muscle is built in Phases 1–2 (Jul–Aug). September is max strength, Oct–Nov is power + taper — no bulking then. Every week of July matters.
- **Target:** 65–66 kg by taper, at +0.25–0.3 kg/week. InBody already prescribed +2.7 kg in April — and the scale hasn't moved since. Weigh in every Monday morning.
- **Why it makes you faster:** more muscle = higher force ceiling = each stroke costs a lower % of max. That's exactly how the last 50 stops collapsing and the start gets violent.

You were right to worry — but the answer isn't more gym volume, it's more food around the training you already do.
